The vision

Picture the market at dawn, every agent working from checked numbers

AI agents will research, test and trade at machine speed. The goal is to build the biggest research platform in finance and quant for them and for the people who direct them, in the open, in five branches: the tools they use, the data they learn from, a library of strategies to build on, an engine that combines them and new methods that keep them honest. Flagship funds from the US are where the best of it is meant to trade.

Five branches, one destination

What it stands on

Financial software was built for people at terminals. More and more of the work is now done by AI agents. Each branch feeds the others: developers come for the tools and data and build in the library, the most different strategies feed ALPHAC, the methods keep every result honest, and the funds' record brings more developers. Everything is open source and published as it is built, including the tests that fail; each branch says what exists today and what comes next, and a goal stays a goal until it is built and measured.

MCP servers and API

02

Data and annotation

Started

Financial datasets to train and test AI models, computed from public filings and checked by qualified people. The aim is the best expert annotation in finance.

Today

  • FilingFacts asks AI models questions about what companies reported to the SEC. On their own, gpt-5-mini answered 19% correctly; with a Canli MCP server reading the filings, 72%.
  • Every answer is recomputed by an independent checker and cites its filing. No item is called human-verified until two people have checked it.

Next

The first double-labelled expert gold set, a paid network of qualified annotators, and benchmark results for more models.

03

The sleeve library

Started

A library where developers and their agents build, train and test millions of strategy sleeves, with honest statistics on every result. Failed experiments are kept and used, and developers get their own testing servers later.

Today

  • canli-quant-mcp ships a library of strategy sleeves with backtests and multiple-testing corrections, so a search over many sleeves is judged as a search.
  • Every trial ALPHAC runs is counted and published, including the ones that fail.

Next

Version 1 of the training environment, open to developers, growing from today's library toward millions of sleeves.

04

ALPHAC, the breadth engine

Paper only

One book built from hundreds of thousands of sleeves. What matters most is how little they move together: the number of independent bets, not the number of sleeves, sets the ceiling, so a sleeve may be weak as long as it is weakly correlated with the rest and clears its own costs. The target is a Sharpe ratio far beyond 2, claimed only on a forward record after costs.

Today

  • ALPHAC, the open-source engine, keeps its paper-traded record, its trial accounting and its retracted figures in public.
  • Its research found that many sleeves built on the same few ideas add almost nothing, so the work now is new, uncorrelated families: new markets, horizons and kinds of data.

Next

Ideas admitted as whole families on their correlation to the book, more asset classes, and a forward record long enough to tell skill from luck.

05

The breakthroughs lab

Started

New mathematics and statistics for quant research: tests that tell a real edge from luck, published openly so anyone can check them.

Today

  • The validators (deflated Sharpe, probability of backtest overfitting, minimum track record, the haircut Sharpe and White's Reality Check) are checked against their papers. They measure overfitting risk; they cannot remove it.
  • Studies of strategies with no edge at all decide which tests the servers run, and the methods ship with their code.

Next

Papers for peer review, and new tests built for the way AI agents now search through strategies.
