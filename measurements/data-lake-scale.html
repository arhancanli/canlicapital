Data lake scale

By Arhan Canli, Canli Capital

Data lake scale is one of 89 measurements this engine publishes in full. It is regenerated from a real run rather than transcribed, and it is shown here with its own claim boundary so the number and its limits arrive together.

What this measurement does and does not claim

Counts what is on disk and what pytest collects. It says nothing about data quality, coverage gaps, or whether any of it supports a trading claim; only how much there is. Runs no backtest, opens no return data, registers no hypothesis. 0 trials.

Corrections this measurement forced

  • '392K+ point-in-time fundamentals' was OVERSTATED; the true figure is 380,878. An overstated count is the only kind of staleness that matters.
  • '8,436 US stocks, survivorship-free' matched neither store and has been replaced by the point-in-time membership count, which is what the phrase means.
  • '3.5M+ hourly bars' and '2,820+ automated tests' were understated by enough to mislead in the other direction; the '+' made them true and uninformative.

Why each figure carries a definition. 'How many US stocks' has at least two defensible answers here and they differ by 2.6x: 18,015 instruments have bars, and 6,835 have ever been in the point-in-time universe. Publishing one without saying which it is would replace a stale number with an ambiguous one.
